How to Use PenguinCAM

PenguinCAM runs as a panel inside Onshape. You build a cutting job step by step in a simple wizard: set up the machine and tool, add one or more parts, arrange them on your stock, then preview and download the G-code.

ℹ️
Two ways to run it:
  • Inside Onshape (recommended): The panel imports parts directly from your model when you click a face — nothing to export or upload.
  • Standalone at /app: The same wizard, but you add parts by uploading DXF files. Use this if you don't work in Onshape.

The Wizard at a Glance

Whichever way you launch it, PenguinCAM walks you through the same steps. The step bar at the top shows where you are; use Back and Next to move between steps.

Step What you do
1. Setup Choose the machine, cutting mode, tool diameter, and material.
2. Parts Add parts — click faces in Onshape, or upload DXF files.
3. Layout Position, rotate, and flip parts on the stock sheet. (2D mode only.)
4. Preview & Save Review the 3D toolpath, then download or save to Google Drive.
ℹ️
The Layout step only appears in 2D mode. In 2.5D and Tubing modes the job is a single part, so the wizard goes straight from Parts to Preview.

Getting Started in Onshape

Install the App

Add PenguinCAM to your Onshape account from the Onshape App Store. This docks a PenguinCAM panel to the side of your Onshape window. Open a Part Studio, then open the panel to start a job.

Connect to Onshape

The first time you open the panel, you'll see a Connect to Onshape button. Click it, complete the sign-in in the popup window, and approve access. This lets PenguinCAM read the geometry of the faces you select. You only need to do this once per session.

⚠️
Tip: If drawings are visible in your Part Studio, hide them first. You need to select the actual solid body face, not a drawing.

Step 1 — Setup

Configure the job before adding parts. These settings pin down feeds and speeds, the tool kerf, and how thickness is handled, so they must be chosen first.

Cutting Mode

Pick the mode that matches what you're cutting:

  • 2D — flat plate (one or more parts): Simple flat patterns cut from sheet stock. You enter the material thickness. Supports multiple parts per job.
  • 2.5D — derive thickness from CAD (single part): Parts with pockets, grooves, countersinks, or raised bosses at multiple depths. Thickness is read automatically from your CAD model. One part per job.
  • Tubing — machine both faces of a tube: Patterns cut into aluminum tube. See Tube Machining below.

Machine, Tool & Material

Setting Description
Machine Which CNC to use. Only shown if your team has more than one machine configured.
Tool diameter End mill diameter in inches (e.g., 0.157 for 4mm). Also used as the kerf/clearance when arranging parts.
Material Material preset (plywood, aluminum, etc.). Sets feed rates and depth of cut.
Material thickness Stock thickness in inches. Shown in 2D mode. Hidden in 2.5D — it's derived from your CAD part.
ℹ️
Changing mode later: Switching between 2D and 2.5D clears the parts list, since they have different rules. The wizard warns you before doing this.

Step 2 — Parts

Adding parts from Onshape

With the panel on the Parts step, the status reads "Select a face in Onshape…". Simply click a planar face in your model. PenguinCAM imports it automatically — there is no "send" button and nothing to export. The status briefly shows "Importing face…", then the part appears in the list and selection re-arms so you can immediately pick another face.

Each part in the list shows a small thumbnail of its outline, its name, and its dimensions (e.g. side panel — 6.0 x 4.0"). Click the × next to a part to remove it.

Adding parts by upload (standalone)

In the standalone app at /app, the Parts step shows a drop zone instead. Drag a DXF file onto it, or click to browse. Export your DXF from CAD as a top-down view looking straight down at the part.

How many parts?

  • 2D: Add as many parts as fit on your sheet. Repeated adds of the same face are numbered (#2, #3…) so you can tell copies apart.
  • 2.5D: One part per job. Remove the current part before adding a different one.
  • Tubing: Add one face (mirrored onto the opposite side of the tube) or two faces (a distinct pattern per side).

Step 3 — Layout (2D mode)

The Layout step is a top-down view of your stock with every part on it. The dotted box is the stock; its combined size is shown in the upper-right, and its lower-left corner is the G54 origin (0,0), marked with a green dot. The info line shows your machine's bed size and tool kerf.

↔
Widen the panel for more room to work. Layout is easier with a wider panel or in the standalone app.

Arranging parts

  • Select: Click a part to select it. Shift-click to select several at once. Click empty space to deselect.
  • Move: Drag a selected part (or group) to reposition it.
  • Rotate: Drag the round handle above a selected part. Rotation is free, but snaps to 45° increments when you're close to one. Hold Shift to disable the snap.
  • Flip over: Click Flip over to mirror the selected part(s). A (flipped) label reminds you to physically turn that piece over before clamping it.
  • Zoom: Use the − and + buttons to zoom the view.

Live validation

The canvas checks your layout as you work. Parts turn red and an error appears if they:

  • Overlap, or sit closer than one tool diameter apart (they'd collide during cutting), or
  • Extend beyond your machine's bed.

You can't move on to Preview until the layout is valid.

ℹ️
Single part? The Layout step still works — just position the one part at the origin. In 2.5D and tubing modes there's no Layout step at all.

Step 4 — Preview & Save

PenguinCAM generates the G-code automatically when you reach this step. You'll see the part/face count and an estimated cycle time, plus an interactive 3D preview.

3D preview

  • Stock: A box showing your material dimensions.
  • Toolpaths: The moves the tool will make, with each part rendered in position on the sheet.
  • Origin: (0,0,0) is the lower-left corner at the sacrifice board.
  • Orbit & zoom: Drag to rotate the view, scroll to zoom, and use Reset view to recenter.

Scrubbing and playback

Drag the scrubber to step through the program move by move — it shows "Move X of Y" and the current operation (e.g. Profile Cut). Use the play/pause and restart controls to animate the tool, and the speed dropdown (0.25×–8×) to slow down or speed up the animation.

Download & save

Click Download Program to save the G-code (.nc) to your computer. If your team has Google Drive configured, click the caret (▼) next to the button and choose Send to Google Drive to upload it straight to your team's shared folder.

Tab Strategies

PenguinCAM offers two ways to keep parts secured while the perimeter is cut. You choose the strategy in your team's config file.

  • Automatic tabs (default): Small tabs are placed automatically along the perimeter. The program cuts the perimeter leaving tabs intact, then returns at the end to remove them. Safest option; no extra fixturing needed.
  • Pause for fixturing: No tabs. The program pauses (M0) before the perimeter cut so you can fixture the part through freshly-milled holes with screws or clamps. Gives a cleaner edge but needs manual intervention.

Tube Machining

Tubing mode cuts hole patterns into aluminum tube, machining both faces so the pattern goes all the way through. Choose Tubing as the mode in Setup, then set:

  • Tube height: Distance from the bottom to the top of the tube in your CNC jig.
  • Square the tube end before machining: Faces off the near end so it's square.
  • Machine the far end to length: Cuts the tube to final length.

On the Parts step, add faces for the tube:

  • One face: The pattern is mirrored onto the opposite side, so both sides match.
  • Two faces: Each side gets its own distinct pattern — useful when one side needs larger access holes for bolt heads.

The program will:

  • Face the first half of the tube,
  • Pause and prompt you to flip the tube 180° end-for-end,
  • Face the second half to complete the pattern (mirrored so the holes line up).

Understanding the Output

G-code Header Comments

Every generated program starts with setup instructions:

  • Stock size required
  • Tool diameter used
  • Material settings
  • Origin location (bottom-left corner, Z=0 at the sacrifice board)

Coordinate System

  • X-axis: Increases to the right (looking at the machine)
  • Y-axis: Increases away from you
  • Z-axis: Z=0 at the sacrifice board, positive is UP
  • Origin (0,0): Bottom-left corner of your stock (the lower-left of the layout)

Work Coordinate System

Programs use G54 work coordinates. Set your work zero on the CNC at:

  • X=0, Y=0 at the bottom-left corner of the stock
  • Z=0 at the top of the sacrifice board (not the stock top)

Troubleshooting

The panel says "Import failed"

Make sure you clicked a flat, planar face on a solid body — not a drawing, edge, or curved surface. Hide any visible drawings, then click the face again (selection stays armed, so no button to press). If it keeps failing, check that you completed the Connect to Onshape sign-in.

Parts don't fit on the stock

The layout box turns red when parts exceed the machine bed. To fix it:

  • Rotate or re-nest parts to pack them tighter
  • Remove parts and cut them in a second job
  • Use a larger machine (configure it in your team config file)

Parts show as overlapping

Parts must stay at least one tool diameter apart so the cutter can pass between them. Drag them farther apart until the red highlight clears.

Holes aren't round

Make sure your tool diameter is smaller than the hole. PenguinCAM warns you about unmillable features if the tool is too large.

Tabs are in bad locations

Tabs are placed automatically based on perimeter length. You can't manually position them, but they're designed to be removed easily with a file or grinder. Alternatively, use the "pause for fixturing" option in your team config to skip tabs entirely and fixture through milled holes instead.

Multi-layer part has incorrect depths

In 2.5D mode, depths are measured down from the top of the stock, so they only come out right if the stock and the machine zero match your CAD. The two most common causes of wrong depths are:

  • Stock thickness doesn't match the CAD. Your physical stock must be the same thickness as the part in Onshape. If it's thicker or thinner, every layer is off by that difference.
  • Z=0 isn't set at the bottom of the stock. PenguinCAM cuts to Z=0 at the sacrifice board, so set your machine's work zero at the bottom of the stock (top of the sacrifice board), not the top surface.

If the stock and machine zero are both correct but depths still look wrong:

  • Check for unexpected faces at odd depths in your CAD
  • Make sure you selected a face on the top-most layer of the part
  • Hide any construction geometry or drawings before selecting
